## 4. D-DIR-3 — `analystScore.direction` stays the analyst's own verdict; no submitted-side feedback

**Decision.** The two direction surfaces are **different statements and remain so**: `scoredSignal.direction` (the record) carries the **submitted side** — an ingest fact, per the governed contract's "aligned with USS v1.1 facts.direction"; `analystScore.direction` carries the **analyst's own conclusion**, derived from the analyst's inputs (`weeklyBias`/`dailyBias`, today hardcoded neutral), and is **not modified by this program** — it remains `"neutral"` in HTTP responses and inside the embedded analyst score until the analyst gains real directional inputs under separate governance. Feeding the submitted side into `weeklyBias`/`dailyBias` (or any analyst bias input) is expressly **not authorized**: it would trip the aligned-bias structure term (`afi-core/analysts/froggy.trend_pullback_v1.ts:60-62`, +0.4 structure ⇒ +0.1 `uwrScore`), granting every signal a free lift merely for asserting a side, and it would collapse the analyst/provider role split (owner ruling 2026-07-30: the analyst configures enrichment and rubric; the signal provider feeds opportunities and configures nothing). Disagreement between the two fields is **signal, not defect** — it is precisely the record of what the analyst concluded independently of what the submitter asserted.

**Scope-guard.** Semantics record only; changes no analyst code and no schema text. Future higher-timeframe bias wiring — the act that would make the analyst's verdict genuinely directional, and that must first resolve the `"unknown"`-direction branch semantics (`trend_pullback_v1.ts:189-192`) — remains a separate, owner-gated mission outside this decision.
