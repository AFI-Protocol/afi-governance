## 1. Evidence basis (inline; file:line citations are input, not authority)

**1.1 The retired layer is entirely dormant.** The deployed scoring path has never contained challenge **logic**: the only challenge tokens under `afi-reactor/src` are the two retired `lifecycleState` enum members and their description recital inside the vendored EV3 closure (`src/pipeline/governed-schema/scored-signal-evidence.v3.schema.json:102,:109`), which D-CHR-6(2) removes; no executable path reads them. No persisted record carries a challenge state — the only `lifecycleState` ever written is `SCORED`. The deployed Base Sepolia contracts (`AFIToken`, `AFISignalReceipt`, `AFIMintCoordinator`) contain no challenge logic; the sole challenge "contract" was a five-line empty stub deleted 2026-08-03 under the disposition already recorded at `afi-docs/specs/AFI_V0_DEPRECATION_AND_MIGRATION.md:90` (row A8). The challenge implementation that exists — `afi-mint/src/orchestrator/{ChallengeWindowManager,ChallengeSubmitter,DisputeResolver}.ts` plus `src/snapshot/{ProposalBuilder,VoteResultReader}.ts` — has **zero consumers anywhere in the organization**, no test coverage, and sits in a repository that declares two nonexistent npm dependencies and therefore does not build. **Nothing observable changes when it is removed.**

**1.2 The architectural ground.** A challenge layer resolves disputes over claims that cannot be mechanically verified. AFI's determinations are the opposite: qualification is `decayedScore >= minDecayScoreThreshold` over hash-stamped values, replayable from Evidence V3 by any party (`recordHash`/`replayHash` are verified by recomputation at admission, EV3-GOV D-EV3-7); outcome measurement is exchange candles captured by a scheduled job under the stamped-value law (DH-GOV D-DH-2(1)). There is no subjective claim for a challenger to contest — a disagreement about a hash is a recomputation, not a vote.

**1.3 The temporal contradiction is structural, not a mis-set constant.** The recorded challenge window is **24 hours** (`uwr-profile-pin-v0.1.md:138`); the live decay law is per-signal minutes — froggy's shipped half-lives are 60 minutes (5m chart) and 180 minutes (15m chart) under TDR-GOV D-TDR-4, verified live 2026-08-04. A 24-hour window is 8–24 half-lives: every signal would be fully decayed long before its window closed. Human deliberation and intraday decay operate on incompatible clocks; no choice of constant reconciles them.

**1.4 The trust model the layer presumes does not exist.** Stake, slash, and quorum presume a live token, adversarial participants, and real voters. The protocol has one registered analyst, a reserved (unminted) token, and an empty Snapshot space. The economics were never governed: LIFE-GOV expressly reserved challenge economics to CHAIN-GOV (`lifecycle-v0.1.md:23`, `:124`) and no such filing exists, so the shipped constants (`minChallengeStake` 100, `challengeSlashRate` 0.5, `slashRewardRate` 0.8, `challengeSuccessThreshold` 0.66, `minDisputeQuorumThreshold` 0.1 — `afi-mint/src/orchestrator/types.ts:200-216`) appear in **no accepted decision**.

**1.5 Native quality control already exists in the accepted architecture.** Measured outcomes (DH-GOV/DHP-GOV/TDR-GOV, live), PoI/PoInsight analyst-capability metrics (owner ruling 2026-07-25; invariants live in afi-core, formulas reserved), and registry admission/removal (`registries/analyst-strategies/README.md` change-control; FCP-GOV D-FCP-5's `status: inactive` retirement path). A defective analyst is **measured and delisted**, not out-voted. This is the AFI-shaped answer to "what if a score is wrong," and it is already the design.

**1.6 Doctrinal drift already recorded.** LIFE-GOV ratified the challenge phase on **2026-07-14**; CONST-GOV retired DAO-as-current eleven days later on **2026-07-25** (`constitutional-architecture-v1.0.md`), while the layer's engine — Snapshot voting, stake, slash, quorum — is DAO machinery. The layer was left standing across that shift. Retiring it aligns the lifecycle with the constitution rather than against it.

## 2. D-CHR-1 — the retirement ruling

**Decision.** The **challenge layer is retired in its entirety**: the challenge phase of the canonical lifecycle, its states, its designated authority, its window, its dispute/appeal process, and its stake/slash/quorum economics are **not part of the AFI protocol** and will not be implemented. The retirement is prospective and total within the scopes this decision holds; every artifact implementing, specifying, or advertising the layer is removed under D-CHR-6 rather than left as dormant surface.

The recorded grounds are §1.1–§1.6: the layer is unimplemented and unconsumed (§1.1); AFI's determinations are mechanically verifiable, so adversarial dispute resolution has no object (§1.2); its window and the shipped decay law are on structurally incompatible clocks (§1.3); its economics presume a trust model that does not exist and were never governed (§1.4); the architecture already carries a native, measurement-based quality-control path (§1.5); and the layer predates the constitutional retirement of DAO-as-current (§1.6).

**Scope-guard.** Retires the challenge layer only. It does not retire, weaken, or re-open qualification, certification, finalization, maturity hold, escrow, the evidence plane, the registry admission/removal path, or any DAO surface; it implements nothing and wires nothing.

## 3. D-CHR-2 — the amended canonical lifecycle machine

**Decision.** LIFE-GOV D-LIFE-1's canonical machine is amended, prospectively, to:

`INGESTED → VALIDATED → SCORED → CERTIFIED → QUALIFIED → FINALIZED → EPOCH_ELIGIBLE`

with terminal/reject branches `SCHEMA_REJECTED` (validation fail), `DECERTIFIED` (certification fail), `UNQUALIFIED` (qualification fail), and `FINAL_REJECTED` (redefined below). Specifically:

1. **`CHALLENGE_OPEN` and `CONTESTED` are retired** as canonical lifecycle states. They have no writer, no reader, and no persisted instance; retiring them removes no data and breaks no record.
2. **The seven-phase separation becomes six** (superseding `lifecycle-v0.1.md:17`): schema validation · scoring · certification · qualification · finalization · epoch eligibility. **D-LIFE-2 phase 5 (challenge) is retired in whole** (`:65`), and **phase 6's trigger is redefined** (`:66`): finalization is no longer triggered by "the challenge window closed" but by *qualification completing and the maturity hold elapsing*.
3. **`FINALIZED` is redefined** (superseding D-LIFE-4's definitional phrase at `:91`): from "the challenge window has closed and the decision is now immutable" to **"qualification is complete and the maturity hold has elapsed; the lifecycle decision is now immutable."** Everything else in D-LIFE-4 stands unchanged and is expressly re-affirmed: exactly one finality writer; the finality writer is distinct from the scorer, the certifier, and the qualifier (the enumeration simply no longer includes a challenge authority); finalization is lifecycle finality, **not** settlement finality; mint remains strictly downstream of finalization (the LIFE-07 correction stands); and the immutability rule and its scope-guard (`:93`) are untouched.
4. **`FINAL_REJECTED` is redefined, not retired** (superseding `lifecycle-v0.1.md:49`): from "rejected at/after the challenge window" to **"terminal rejection of a signal that had passed qualification — reached only by objective decertification (D-CHR-7(2))."** The state remains necessary: `UNQUALIFIED` records a qualification failure and cannot express a post-qualification defect. It remains a finality-writer-only transition under D-LIFE-4.
5. **The challenge authority ceases to be a designated transition owner** (superseding the D-LIFE-3 table row at `:81`). The remaining designated roles — scorer, certifier, qualifier, finality writer, epoch-eligibility owner — are unchanged, and D-LIFE-3's separation-of-duties principle is re-affirmed over the smaller set. LIFE-GOV §9's open items are unaffected: it names only "naming and wiring the finality writer and the epoch-eligibility owner" and never reserved a challenge-authority wiring slot.
6. **Tier-4 reconciliation recitals become stale, and are never rewritten.** `lifecycle-v0.1.md:33` and `:51` record the three shipped vocabularies and map `afi-mint challenge_window`/`dispute_resolved` and `afi-infra challenge_open`/`voting_complete` onto the canonical machine, and `uwr-profile-pin-v0.1.md:36` recites the `validatorConfig` challenge default. Those recitals described tier-4 reality at their authoring time; the reality is removed by D-CHR-6. Per the standing no-rewrite law they stay byte-frozen in their accepted files and are **superseded as fact by this clause**.

**Scope-guard.** Amends the state machine, the phase list, two state definitions, and one authority row only. It creates no writer for any state, wires no phase, changes no storage or evidence law beyond the enum amendment of D-CHR-5, and does not re-open any other D-LIFE clause — D-LIFE-1's remaining states, D-LIFE-5, D-LIFE-6, and the §8 reservations all stand.

## 4. D-CHR-3 — UP-9's challenge-window value is void; the profile stays byte-untouched

**Decision.** The `challengeWindowDurationHours: 24` value recorded by UP-9 (`uwr-profile-pin-v0.1.md:52`, `:138`) is **void prospectively**: it records a duration for a phase that no longer exists and is of no force. **The pinned profile document `afi-config/registries/uwr-profiles/uwr-weighted-lifts-v0.1.json` and its schema `schemas/uwr-profile/v0/uwr-profile.schema.json` are left BYTE-UNTOUCHED by this decision**, for three recorded reasons:

1. **UP-5 forbids mutating the pinned profile in place** (`uwr-profile-pin-v0.1.md:105`): any change is a new profile version with a new id, never a mutation of `uwr-weighted-lifts-v0.1`.
2. **The field is structurally inert.** The profile is not read in production (`AFI_UWR_PROFILE_SOURCE` is set in no deploy artifact) and, decisively, the RC-5 loader returns `defaultUwrConfig` regardless of the document's contents (`afi-core/validators/UwrProfileLoader.ts:207`), so no profile value can reach runtime. Voiding the value changes no behavior; removing the bytes would change no behavior either.
3. **Removing it now would be disproportionate.** The field is `required` under `additionalProperties: false` (`uwr-profile.schema.json:179`) with a `const: 24` pin (`:183`), asserted by the afi-config pin tests (`tests/uwr-profile-schema-validation.test.ts:247`, `:561`), mirrored in the byte-identical example (`:48`), pinned in an afi-core loader test (`:74`), and the profile is referenced by `profileId` from the canonical-hash KAT fixtures. Physical removal therefore forces a **profile re-version cascade** — new `profileId`, the reactor's `PINNED_UWR_PROFILE_ID` and boot pin, the RC-5 identity predicate, and every pin site above — for zero behavioral gain.

**The physical removal of the field rides the next profile re-version**, which other accepted work already requires, and is authorized by *that* filing rather than this one. Until then the value stands in the byte-frozen document as **recorded-but-void residue**.

**Scope-guard.** Voids one recorded value. It changes no profile byte, no schema byte, no weight, axis, `profileId`, KAT, or pin; it does not authorize a profile re-version, and it does not touch UP-9's `minDecayScoreThreshold` or qualification `rule`, which stand.

## 8. D-CHR-7 — what is reserved, and what is protected

**Decision.**

1. **Objective divergence detection is RESERVED** as the replacement surface, should redundant evaluation ever be wanted. If multiple parties ever score the same signal, disagreement is detected by **recomputation and automatic divergence flagging** — a measurement, never a vote, never a stake. No implementation, threshold, or authority is decided here; a future filing holds this slot.
2. **Operator/Institute decertification on objective grounds is RESERVED.** The fraud and defect cases the challenge layer nominally covered — hash mismatch, corrupted feed, provably fabricated provenance — are handled by decertification on **objective, recomputable** grounds, producing `DECERTIFIED` (pre-qualification) or `FINAL_REJECTED` (post-qualification, D-CHR-2(4)). Its authority, procedure, and evidentiary standard are **not decided here**; INST-GOV's operator posture is untouched and no new authority is conferred.
3. **The DAO's Snapshot infrastructure is expressly PROTECTED, not retired.** The owner records that the Snapshot space and `afidao.eth` were established for **gasless voting across all DAO activity**, not for challenging signals. The space, the ENS name, the generic `SnapshotClient`, and `validatorConfig.schema.json`'s `snapshotSpaceId` (default `afidao.eth`, described there as the canonical AFI DAO space) all **survive untouched**. Only the challenge/dispute **use** of Snapshot is retired. Nothing in this decision constrains the DAO's future design, which remains reserved-future per CONST-GOV D-CONST-4.

**Scope-guard.** Reserves two surfaces and protects one. It implements none of them, confers no authority, sets no threshold, and decides no DAO question.
