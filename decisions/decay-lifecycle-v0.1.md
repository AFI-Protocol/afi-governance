## 1. D-DLC-1 — The ratio law is the canonical decay engine (closes UP-8)

**Decision.** The canonical decay law of the protocol is the exponential half-life law over minutes, exactly as every determination already stamps it: `decayed(t) = sealedScore × 0.5^(elapsedMinutes / halfLifeMinutes)`, where `halfLifeMinutes` comes from the determination's stamped `decayParams` and from nowhere else. All three registration forms of `decayConfig` (template ref, inline, ratio) **compile to a `halfLifeMinutes` value consumed by this one law** — the ratio form via `barsPerHalfLife × timeframeMinutes` (TDR-GOV D-TDR-1, ratified unchanged), the template form via the preset's declared `halfLifeMinutes`, the inline form verbatim. There is no second engine: `thetaBias` and every other template member beyond `halfLifeMinutes` are **annotational** until a future filing gives one of them operational meaning. The half-life **unit question is settled as minutes** (derived bars × timeframe for the ratio form) — the applier's legacy hour-unit surface conforms or is superseded (D-DLC-3).

**The sealed score never changes.** Decay is *predictable change*: a deterministic function of elapsed time declared at scoring. The decayed value is DERIVED, never stored into the record, never hashed, and never a substitute for the sealed score in any provenance or evidence surface. It is recomputable by anyone from the sealed record **plus the registered configuration its `analystConfigHash` pins** (the stamp surfaces are the scored payload, HTTP response, and operational scoring-context document — TDR-GOV's recorded placement; the V3 evidence record carries no `decayParams` and continues to carry none).

**Scope-guard.** This clause ratifies live TDR-GOV behavior and moves zero bytes. It does not amend the stamp shape, its placement, the parsing law, the backstop, or the capture-horizon law.

**The false surface, named.** The profile document's `decaySurface.horizonSelection` machinery selects nothing at runtime — selection is per-strategy via `decayConfig`, and only the `unknownOrMissing → decay-swing-v1` arm survives as the declared-nothing backstop mirror (TDR-GOV D-TDR-1(4)). ## 3. D-DLC-3 — The applier is re-wired as a read-side derivation (the visible dynamic process)

**Decision.** The dormant applier surface becomes the protocol's single production decay derivation, with these binding properties:
1. **Read-side only.** The derivation runs at the scored-signal read/serving surface — the existing HTTP read responses and surfaces presenting them — never inside the scoring pipeline, never as a pipeline node, and never as a write. It computes from exactly three inputs: the sealed score, the determination's stamped `decayParams.halfLifeMinutes` (equivalently re-derivable from the registered configuration the record's `analystConfigHash` pins), and elapsed time since the sealed scoring timestamp. Creating any NEW public read endpoint or participant-gateway surface is outside this filing.
2. **Fail-closed on the stamp.** A determination without stamped decay params yields **no derived decay** — no default half-life, no unit guess. The silent `DEFAULT_SIGNAL_HALF_LIFE_HOURS = 24` fallback (`SignalDecay.ts:27`) is retired from every production path; the hour-unit signature conforms to the minutes-based canonical law or is superseded by a minutes-based entry point, and the sole existing caller (`ValidatorDecision.ts:71`, dormant) conforms or is superseded in the same slot. This is the full-fidelity doctrine applied to decay: a parameter the analyst never chose is never quietly substituted.
3. **Zero sealed-byte movement.** The derivation is never persisted into any evidence record, enters no hash preimage, and moves no golden. The visible surface (API field, UI meter, or both) is presentation of a derivation, labeled as derived. The visible surface rests on the owner's 2026-08-23 visible-decay ruling recorded in the Status line — the newer authorization; TDR-GOV §0's recorded readout gate ("≥150 one-law rows/horizon") continues to govern outcome-quality readouts and is not lifted by this filing, and the 2026-08-03 "front-end act: none until last" recording is superseded **for this bounded derived-decay surface only** by the newer ruling (§7).
4. **Volatility/conviction adjustment stays dormant.** `applyVolatilityAdjustedDecay` and `calculateAdjustedHalfLife` are expressly NOT wired by this clause — an adjusted half-life is a different declared parameter, which only a future filing may introduce.

**Scope-guard.** This clause authorizes the derivation semantics; the realizing code lands only through the §6 slot and its gate.

## 4. D-DLC-4 — Half-life checkpoint semantics (what the validator checks, and what a reassessment is)

**Decision.**
1. **The checkpoint moment.** A scored signal becomes checkpoint-eligible at its stamped half-life (`elapsedMinutes ≥ halfLifeMinutes`); eligibility is a derived time predicate, not a lifecycle state or transition. The capture rows at {H/4, H/2, H} (DH-GOV law, unchanged) are the checkpoint's evidence. The checkpoint CONSUMES the existing capture; it changes nothing about how outcomes are measured or written.
2. **What is checked.** The checkpoint reads exactly: the sealed record (assertion: direction, plan, sealed score, stamped decay clock) and that signal's `signal_outcomes` rows (realization: entry/exit-window returns per horizon). The reassessment is the comparison of assertion against realization for that one signal — `signal_outcomes` gains its first reader.
3. **The reassessment is a sealed artifact.** A reassessment NEVER mutates the scored record (CFG-GOV: immutability attaches at `SCORED`). It is a new, separately sealed artifact linked to the signal by `signalId`, carrying **exactly**: the checkpoint time, the horizons read, the realized figures consumed, the reassessment reading, and the `signalId` linkage — **and nothing more without a further filing**. Its governed schema is defined inside the `DLC-CHECKPOINT` slot's PR chain under this clause's authority, in the house one-file-per-artifact discipline, with strict validation and valid/invalid vectors. **Which hash discipline seals the reassessment artifact is a named open question the slot must answer with governed vectors** — more than one canonicalization discipline carries the `afi.hash.v1` stamp in the implementation today, so the slot names its discipline explicitly and proves it, rather than inheriting one silently.
4. **Human or automated, same contract.** Whether a validator is a person or a process, the checkpoint contract is identical: read sealed assertion + captured realization, emit sealed reassessment. Validator identity, eligibility, and any role authority are NOT decided here — they belong with auth/tenancy, and any reserved `CHAIN-GOV` role ground is honored (§7).
5. **Reputation is the follow-on, by name.** Per-analyst aggregation of reassessments over time — the calibration record — is `DLC-REPUTATION`, a named slot whose semantics need their own clause set once reassessments exist to aggregate. Nothing else may claim its ground.

**Scope-guard.** No capture change, no evidence-v3 change, no new member on the scored record, no lifecycle state or transition, no mint/qualification consequence. The reassessment artifact is additive and lives beside, never inside, the scored record.
