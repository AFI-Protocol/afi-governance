## 1. D-CAL-1 — The calibration record

**Decision.**
1. **What it is.** `afi.analyst-calibration.v1` is a **new, separately sealed, append-only artifact** — the per-analyst aggregation of that analyst's sealed `afi.signal-reassessment.v1` artifacts. It lives **beside, never inside**, the scored record and the reassessment; it never mutates either (CFG-GOV D-CFG-2: immutability attaches at `SCORED`; D-DLC-4(3): the reassessment's member set is exhaustive and gains nothing here). **Same contract, any producer:** a reassessment is consumed identically whether the checkpoint job or a human validator emitted it (D-DLC-4(4)); and the record is **recomputable by anyone** from the sealed inputs alone (D-DLC-1's principle) — every member is a deterministic function of the sealed reassessments and sealed evidence records it names.
2. **Provenance law — the two-plane join, verified on read.** Every record is computed from exactly two governed sources, both read-only: the reassessment artifacts (analytics plane) and, for each `signalId`, the **sealed** canonical evidence record (evidence plane, read-only credential per D-CFG-2(3), the checkpoint job's custody pattern). From the sealed record, and from nowhere else, come `uwrScore`, `analystId`, `strategyId`, `strategyVersion`, `riskBucket`, `scoredSignal.providerId`, the `composition` members, the `uwrProfile` stamp, `recordHash`, `recordVersion`, and `supersedesRecordHash`; from the reassessment come the readings, the realized figures, `checkpointTime` (`scoredAt`, `checkpointAt`, `halfLifeMinutes`), and `signalId`; `timeframe` and `symbol` are **derived from the `signalId`** under the adapter's recorded identifier format (D-OBJ-1) and carried as `signalId-derived` provenance annotations. Nothing is ever taken from the analytics copy (`scoring_context`), which carries no evidentiary weight (D-CFG-2(4)). **Verify-on-read (D-CFG-2(2)) is a duty of the builder:** for every consumed `signalId` it recomputes the evidence record's `recordHash` and the reassessment's `seal.value`; a `signalId` whose record cannot be read, or whose `recordHash` or `seal.value` does not recompute, is **excluded and counted** under `unverifiableInput`; nothing is substituted or defaulted. (The clock members are taken from the sealed reassessment alone — no sealed-record counterpart exists to check them against; a future filing that stamps the clock on the record may add that check.)
3. **Exhaustive members.** The record carries exactly: `schema` (const `afi.analyst-calibration.v1`); `rule` (const `analyst-calibration-v1`) and `readingRule` (const `signedReturnPct-sign-v1`); `groupKey` (D-CAL-2); `n` (rows aggregated); `attribution` (`corpus` | `analyst`, D-CAL-3(3)); `provenance` — the consumed `signalId`s each with its joined `recordHash` and `recordVersion`, the `[firstScoredAt, lastScoredAt]` window, the `checkpointAt` range, the `signalId`-derived sorted sets `symbols` and `timeframes`, the sorted set `providers`, the sorted set `analystConfigHashes` consumed (D-CAL-2(2)), direction counts (long/short/neutral), and the exclusion counters `unverifiableInput`, `supersededSinceCheckpoint`, `supersededSinceCalibration`, `magnitudeConventionViolation`; `readings` — per horizon fraction `f ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1}`: favorable / adverse / flat / indeterminate counts, and overall confirmed / contradicted / mixed / indeterminate counts, **copied from the governed reading, never re-classified**; `realized` — per `f`: `n` (rows with non-null `signedReturnPct` at that `f` — the denominator of every rate at that `f`), win count (`win` ≡ `perHorizon.outcome == favorable`; flat rows are non-wins) and rate, mean `signedReturnPct`, median `signedReturnPct` (point only; the mean of the two middle values for an even count), mean `mfePct` and `maePct`, each estimate under a `status` (below); `reliability` — per `f`, the twenty `uwrScore` bins of D-CAL-3(2)(b), each with `n`, `favorable`, and its `status`; `rank` — per `f`: Spearman ρ with `n`, its `status`, and — when the point is sealed — `claim` ∈ {`sealed`, `insufficient-for-claim`}; `strata` — the `readings`/`realized` blocks split by `riskBucket` and by direction, each block under its own `status` per D-CAL-3(2)(c). **One status vocabulary for every rate, mean, median, and ρ block** (`realized`, `reliability` bins, `rank`, `strata`): `sealed` (estimate + interval present), `withheld` (the block meets its minimum but `G < 3`: estimate present, interval absent), `insufficient` (below the block's minimum: counts only — no estimate); `dependence` (D-CAL-3(4)); `supersedes` (D-CAL-4); `seal`. **Nothing more without a further filing.** In particular: no composite, no scalar summary, no PoI / PoInsight / Repₜ member or label (D-CAL-5), no cross-key member, no rank across analysts, no comparison between bins.
4. **Deterministic aggregation from per-horizon outcomes.** The record aggregates the reassessment's **per-horizon** outcomes, not its `overall` member alone, because under the governed rule any `flat` horizon forces `overall = mixed`; `overall` counts are carried for completeness but the calibration tables are built from `perHorizon`. A neutral assertion (`signedReturnPct: null`) contributes only to the `indeterminate` and direction counts and to no realized figure — its MFE/MAE are not directional and are not aggregated.
5. **Sealing, rounding, and plane.** The record is sealed by **canonical-json-hashing.v1 (`afi.hash.v1`)**: `seal.value` is the sha256 of the canonical serialization of the document with the single top-level member `seal` excluded; domain tag **`afi.calibration.analyst`** carried, never hashed — the D-DLC-4(3) precedent, named here explicitly and proven by governed valid/invalid vectors in `CAL-SCHEMA`, never inherited silently. So that two conforming implementations seal identically: **every derived non-integer member is sealed rounded to six decimal places (round-half-even, performed on the exact binary value — never via a half-up formatter such as `toFixed`) and compared at that precision; counts are integers; accumulation order is ascending `scoredAt`, then `signalId`; every set-valued provenance member (`signalIds` with their `recordHash`/`recordVersion`, `symbols`, `timeframes`, `providers`, `analystConfigHashes`) is serialized sorted ascending by UTF-16 code units of its key, `signalIds` in accumulation order.** Its store surface is a new analytics-plane collection (`analyst_calibrations`, database `afi_signal_analytics`) beside `signal_reassessments`; **its sole writer is the `CAL-BUILDER` job**; append-only, insert-only, never overwritten (MONGO-GOV D-MONGO-3 honored; D-MONGO-4 plane separation; D-CFG-2(4): the analytics plane is non-canonical — the record is integrity-checkable, not evidentiary).
6. **Magnitude convention.** The calibration schema (`CAL-SCHEMA`) fixes `mfePct`/`maePct` as non-negative magnitudes (production's convention: `capture-outcomes-lib.mjs:143-148`), enforced on consumed rows by the builder as an exclusion rule. A reassessment any of whose non-null-`signedReturnPct` horizons violates `signedReturnPct ≤ mfePct` or `−signedReturnPct ≤ maePct` (non-strict, compared on stored values without rounding) is **excluded and counted** under `magnitudeConventionViolation`. (The governed reassessment *vectors* use a signed `maePct` convention and are therefore not builder fixtures; the 2026-08-24 corpus satisfies the convention on 558/558 rows.)

**Scope-guard.** No edit to the reassessment schema, the scored record, the capture cron, or any hash law that already exists; no lifecycle state or transition; no display; no consequence. The record is additive evidence and nothing else.

## 2. D-CAL-2 — The grouping key and the scoring identity

**Decision.**
1. **Key.** A record is computed per `groupKey = analystId × strategyId@strategyVersion × scoringIdentity × halfLifeMinutes`, with per-`f` sub-tables inside. `halfLifeMinutes` is the governed reassessment member `checkpointTime.halfLifeMinutes` (the sealed reassessment member; no sealed-record counterpart exists); `timeframe` is not a key element because no governed source carries it — it is `signalId`-derived provenance (sealed as the sorted set `provenance.timeframes`). For the parsed arm of a ratio-form registration with fixed `barsPerHalfLife` the half-life determines the timeframe; **precondition recorded, not decided:** assumed-timeframe rows (TDR-GOV D-TDR-1(3)–(4)) and non-ratio registrations (D-TDR-3(2)) are not separable from the sealed inputs, because the `timeframeAssumed` witness lives only in the analytics stamp — a reader detects a mixed set through `provenance.timeframes`, and a future filing that stamps the witness on a sealed artifact may promote the timeframe into the key. **The record never pools keys**, and no member compares two keys.
2. **Scoring identity.** `scoringIdentity` is the tuple of members that pin the scoring instrument on the sealed record: `composition.pipelineId`, `composition.pipelineVersion`, `composition.manifestHash`, `composition.scorerPluginId`, `composition.scorerPluginVersion`, `composition.pluginSetHash`, and the record's `uwrProfile` stamp identity (`profileId`, `source`). It is **not** `analystConfigHash` alone: that hash also covers `decayConfig`, and a decay-only re-registration (TDR-GOV D-TDR-4's own precedent) would fragment calibration partitions with no change to the instrument. The trade-off is recorded: omitting `analystConfigHash` from the identity pools across enrichment-parameter changes that leave the composition members unchanged; the record therefore **also carries** the sorted set of distinct `analystConfigHash` values it consumed (`provenance.analystConfigHashes`), so a reader can split further, and a future filing may promote the hash into the key if the DEM-PRODUCER slots make enrichment parameters score-moving.
3. **Half-life is a key element in its own right:** one config hash spans two live streams today (H = 60 and H = 180), and pooling them would pool two horizon laws.
4. **Eras never pool.** Because a registered configuration version is immutable once scored under (D-CFG-5(1)), a change of instrument is always a new key; the frozen-axis era and any DEM-PRODUCER era are therefore separate records by construction.

**Scope-guard.** This clause names a partition; it moves no hash, adds no stamp to any record, and changes no registration, identifier, or composition law.

## 3. D-CAL-3 — The statistical law

**Decision.**
1. **Estimators — pinned for bit-exact reproduction, no seed.** (i) *Clusters:* each row's window is `[scoredAt, scoredAt + H)` on the reassessment's `scoredAt`; two rows overlap iff `|Δt| < H` (strict); clusters are the connected components of the overlap graph, assigned once per group at H and inherited by every subset (bin, stratum), a subset's `G` being the clusters represented in it. (ii) *Variance:* the CR1 cluster sandwich — `Σ_g (Σ_{i∈g} r_i)² / n² × G/(G−1)`, `r_i` the row residual from the estimate; the design effect is that variance divided by the iid variance `p(1−p)/n` (rates) or `s²/n` (means; `s²` the sample variance, denominator `n−1`). (iii) *Rate and mean intervals:* estimate ± `z₀.₉₇₅` × SE_CR1, rates clipped to [0, 1]. (iv) *ρ:* Pearson correlation of mid-ranks (ties averaged) of `uwrScore` and `signedReturnPct`; `x_i`, `y_i` are the mid-ranks centred on their mean and divided by the population standard deviation (denominator `n`); `IF_i = x_i·y_i − (ρ/2)(x_i² + y_i²)`; `SE_ρ²` is (ii) with `r_i = IF_i`; `SE_z = SE_ρ / (1 − ρ²)`; the interval is `tanh(atanh ρ ± z₀.₉₇₅ · SE_z)` — the Pearson influence function applied to ranks held fixed, adopted as the convention. (v) *Median:* a point only — no interval. (vi) *Few-clusters guard:* any block with `G < 3` carries `status: withheld` (estimate present, interval absent); for `rank`, `G < 3` → `withheld` regardless of `n`, else `n < 200` → `claim: insufficient-for-claim`. Every interval is therefore cluster-robust on the full sample and valid under either sign of within-cluster dependence.
2. **Calibration-scoped minimum-N — a new rule, beside the recorded readout gate.** TDR-GOV §0 / DHP-GOV §0's gate ("≥150 one-law rows/horizon", kept in force by D-DLC-3(3)) **stands untouched**; it governs surfacing `uwrScore` as a quality signal. This clause adds, for the calibration record only: **(a)** a block seals rates, means, and ρ (with intervals) only at `n ≥ 100`; ρ's `claim` is `insufficient-for-claim` below `n ≥ 200` (a round constant; the formula `n = 3 + 1.06·((z₀.₉₇₅ + z₀.₈₀)/atanh ρ)²` gives ≈ 205 independent rows for |ρ| = 0.20 at 80 % power, and under dependence the effective n is the raw n divided by the sealed design effect); below `n ≥ 100` a block seals counts only. **(b)** *Reliability bins:* `[k·0.05, (k+1)·0.05)` for `k = floor(round(uwrScore × 1000) / 50)`, every `k` from 0 to 19 present with its `n` and `favorable`; a bin's `rate` and interval are sealed only at `n ≥ 20` (Wilson half-width ≈ ±0.20 at p = 0.5; the interval sealed is (iii)), otherwise `status: insufficient`; **no comparison between bins is ever a member.** Bin membership depends on `uwrScore` alone, so the sealed bin set is the same at every `f`. **(c)** *Precedence:* rule (a) applies to every rate, mean, median, ρ, and interval wherever it appears — group, stratum, or bin — on that block's own `n`, with (b) as the sole exception for bin rates; "counts" means the integer members plus the dependence block's cluster, pairing, and greedy integers; the design effect and lag-1 autocorrelation are sealed at any `n ≥ 2` provided `G ≥ 3` and `p(1−p) > 0` (resp. `s² > 0`), otherwise withheld. **Under this rule, the record built on the 2026-08-24 corpus seals rates, means, medians, and ρ (`claim: insufficient-for-claim`) for the H = 60 group (n = 141, G = 57), seals the same four reliability bins (k = 7, 8, 10, 11) at every `f`, and seals counts only for the H = 180 group (n = 45, G = 20 — no bin reaches n = 20, no stratum reaches n = 100).**
3. **Attribution rule.** A record whose provenance holds fewer than two distinct symbols **and** whose `lastScoredAt − firstScoredAt` is under 28 days carries `attribution: "corpus"`, not `"analyst"`: its statistics describe that corpus, not the analyst's skill. The member exists so that no reader mistakes one symbol over six days for a track record.
4. **Dependence block.** *Per group:* the overlap-cluster count `G`; the pairing integers `withNeighbour` (rows having any neighbour within `|Δt| ≤ H` on `scoredAt` — non-strict, deliberately unlike the strict cluster rule) and `pairedOpposite` (rows whose nearest such neighbour, ties to the earlier row, is opposite-direction) with `pairingFraction = pairedOpposite / n`; and the greedy non-overlap count under the named rule "ascending `scoredAt`; keep a row iff `scoredAt − lastKept ≥ H`". *Per (group, f):* the design effect of the win rate and the lag-1 autocorrelation of `signedReturnPct` — the Pearson correlation of the `n−1` ordered pairs `(x_i, x_{i+1})` in ascending `scoredAt` within the group. The greedy count is a validity stamp for the reader, not a variance correction.
5. **Direction semantics.** `reassessmentReading.direction` is the sealed assertion's direction, which under DIR-GOV D-DIR-3 is the **submitted side**, not the analyst's own conclusion (today `neutral`). The record therefore measures whether the analyst's *score* predicted the outcome of the provider's side; the schema description states this, and the per-direction strata exist so that a 100 %-single-direction group is visibly market drift.
6. **No scalar, ever.** No member of the record is a single number summarizing an analyst. No shrinkage toward a prior, no composite of rates, no "neutral" default for a new analyst. Counts and intervals are the whole product.

**Scope-guard.** No scoring value, axis, weight, or profile content moves; the readout gate is untouched; no cross-analyst statistic exists; the constants and estimators in this clause bind only the calibration record and may be amended only by a further filing.

## 4. D-CAL-4 — Issuance and supersession

**Decision.**
1. **Append per run.** Each builder run that changes a group's consumed set writes a **new** record for that key carrying `supersedes` = the `seal.value` of the previous record for the same key (or `null` for the first); records are never edited or deleted. The current record for a key is the latest by `supersedes` chain; history is the chain.
2. **Superseded evidence — two decidable rules.** (i) *At checkpoint:* because a governed correction keeps the `signalId` and increments `recordVersion` (D-EV3-1 / D-MONGO-5) while the reassessment carries no hash stamp and no supersession timestamp exists to order the two events, a consumed `signalId` is **excluded and counted** under `supersededSinceCheckpoint` whenever its current sealed record carries `recordVersion > 1` or a `supersedesRecordHash`, or `scored_signal_evidence_history` holds any entry for it — regardless of `checkpointAt`. A future filing that gives the reassessment a `recordHash` stamp, or the record an admission timestamp, may narrow this rule; until then it is deliberately over-inclusive and never silent. (ii) *Since calibration:* each run compares the `(recordHash, recordVersion)` it stamps under `provenance` against the previous calibration record for the same key and **excludes and counts** under `supersededSinceCalibration` any `signalId` whose `recordHash` changed. (No supersession has ever occurred; both rules exist so the first one cannot corrupt a record.)
3. **Per-analyst only.** A run computes every key independently; a record's inputs are the sealed artifacts of that key and nothing from any other key.

**Scope-guard.** No correction law, supersession law, or store-layer immutability rule changes; this clause consumes them.

## 6. D-CAL-6 — afi-benchkit: the disposition

**Decision.**
